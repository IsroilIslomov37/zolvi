A restaurant is a dining room, a kitchen, a storeroom, a delivery service and a finance department all at once. The more paper and manual work in each part, the more money quietly disappears. This article walks through where to start automating a restaurant and in what order to continue.

## Step 1: find where the money leaks

Before choosing any software, spend a week answering a few simple questions:

- How long does an order take to get from the waiter to the kitchen?
- Which products come up short at the end of the month?
- Where are delivery orders recorded?
- How long does it take to know today’s revenue?

The biggest losses usually sit in two places: the link between floor and kitchen, and the storeroom. That is where automation should start.

## Step 2: POS till and kitchen display

The first step is moving orders from paper to digital. The POS till takes the waiter’s order and sends it straight to the kitchen display (KDS). The cook sees the order, preparation time is tracked and late orders are highlighted.

Orders stop getting mixed up, dishes stop going to the wrong table, and the owner sees sales by table and by waiter.

## Step 3: recipe cards and inventory

Each dish gets a recipe card — ingredients and quantities — in the system. When a dish is sold, its ingredients are written off automatically.

That gives you two things:

1. **Shortage control.** You can compare expected and actual stock on any day.
2. **Accurate dish costs.** When ingredient prices rise, you immediately see which dishes stopped making money.

## Step 4: delivery and a Telegram bot

If delivery orders come by phone, Telegram and aggregators, they belong in the same till. A Telegram bot shows customers the menu, takes orders and online payments and sends status updates.

A simple courier app shows where every order is.

## Step 5: customer base and loyalty

Once orders from every channel land in one customer base, you start recognizing regulars: how often they come and what they like. Bonuses and personal offers bring them back more often.

## Step 6: reports and decisions

In the final step data turns into decisions: average check, best- and worst-selling dishes (ABC analysis), load by hour, branch comparison. The owner gets these reports on their phone every morning.

## Common mistakes

- **Rolling out everything at once.** A team cannot learn a new till, inventory and bot simultaneously. Go step by step.
- **Inventory without recipe cards.** Without recipes, stock control is just a list of goods in and out.
- **Skipping staff training.** Being there during the first shifts prevents a lot of trouble later.

## How long does it take?

A POS and kitchen display typically go live in 3–4 weeks, and the full system with recipe cards and inventory in 6–10 weeks. The exact timeline depends on branches and integrations.

## Conclusion

Start restaurant automation where it hurts most — the link between floor and kitchen — then move to inventory and recipe cards, then delivery and the customer base. Each step delivers its own result and lays the groundwork for the next.
