Many companies meet CRM through amoCRM — and that is a sensible choice: fast, affordable and easy to understand. Over time, though, a question appears: “Do we need our own CRM?” This article looks at when to stay on an off-the-shelf CRM, when to move to your own system and how to do it safely.

## What amoCRM does well

amoCRM is built around a sales pipeline: a lead arrives, a manager works it, a deal closes. If your sales process looks like that, amoCRM gives you a lot straight away:

- launch within days;
- a ready mobile app and messenger integrations;
- extensions through a widget marketplace;
- a start without a development budget.

For a small sales team this is often the most reasonable choice, and we say so during the audit.

## 6 signs you have outgrown an off-the-shelf CRM

1. **Spreadsheets multiply outside the CRM.** Groups, schedules, inventory or payments live in separate Excel files because the CRM has no place for them.
2. **Widgets clash.** Every new need means a new widget, and sometimes they break each other.
3. **The subscription has become a real cost.** As the team grows, the monthly fee becomes a visible part of the budget.
4. **Integrations are manual.** Exchanging data with 1C or payment systems is still somebody’s job.
5. **Reports are built in Excel.** The CRM cannot show the metrics the owner needs.
6. **The process bends to the CRM.** “The system won’t let us do that” is a phrase you hear often.

If three or more sound familiar, it is time to think about your own system.

## The third way: hybrid

You do not always have to leave amoCRM entirely. Often the sales pipeline stays in amoCRM and a custom module is added through its API:

- groups and attendance for a training center;
- inventory and stock for a retailer;
- automatic Click and Payme payment matching;
- a Telegram bot for customers.

This keeps costs down and lets the team keep a familiar tool. If a full move is needed later, it becomes easier.

## A safe plan for moving to your own CRM

1. **Audit.** Current process, widgets, integrations and a list of manual tasks.
2. **Prototype.** A clickable prototype of the new CRM that the team tries before development.
3. **First module.** Usually requests and the customer base; it goes live in week 3–4.
4. **Data migration.** Customers, deals, tasks, notes and files, with duplicates cleaned up.
5. **Parallel running.** For a short period both systems stay open so nothing is lost.
6. **Full switch and training.** The old subscription is cancelled and the team works in the new system.

## Conclusion

amoCRM is a good start. Your own CRM pays off when processes no longer fit the template, users multiply and deep integrations become necessary. In many cases the best route is hybrid: stay on amoCRM and fill the gaps with a custom module.
