“How much does a CRM cost?” is the question we hear most. The honest answer: CRM cost depends on several factors, and an “average price” tells you almost nothing. This article explains what makes up the cost of a CRM, how off-the-shelf and custom CRMs differ, and how to calculate the cost over three years.

## What CRM cost is made of

Any CRM — off-the-shelf or custom — has three cost components:

1. **The software itself** — a monthly subscription for an off-the-shelf CRM, or one-off development for a custom one.
2. **Implementation** — configuration, data migration, integrations and staff training.
3. **Running it** — support, updates, servers and extra modules.

Most people look only at the first part and then run into “hidden” costs. A fair comparison counts all three.

## Off-the-shelf CRM: the subscription model

amoCRM, Bitrix24 and similar products usually charge a monthly subscription per user. Getting started is easy: sign up, configure a pipeline and you are working within days.

Costs of an off-the-shelf CRM:

- a monthly subscription that scales with the number of users;
- extra widgets or apps for telephony, messengers and 1C;
- integrator fees for configuration and integrations;
- rising costs when you move up a plan or the team grows.

## Custom CRM: one-off development

A custom CRM is built for your process, from scratch or from ready modules. The main cost is development, paid once. After that there are no monthly license fees — only servers and, if you want it, support.

What drives the cost of a custom CRM:

| Factor | Effect on price |
| --- | --- |
| Number of modules | Each module (leads, pipeline, payments, inventory) is estimated separately |
| Integrations | 1C, IP telephony, Telegram, Click/Payme — each adds work |
| Roles and branches | Complex permissions and branches mean more development and testing |
| Data migration | How much needs moving from old spreadsheets and systems |
| Mobile app | If sales agents or couriers need a dedicated app |

## Hidden costs

Whichever route you take, budget for:

- **Cleaning and migrating data.** A customer list in Excel is usually full of duplicates and errors.
- **Staff training.** Even the best CRM is useless if managers do not use it.
- **Integrations.** A CRM without telephony and 1C only half works.
- **Time to change processes.** The team may be slower in the first month.

## How to calculate the three-year cost

The fairest way to compare off-the-shelf and custom CRM is the total cost of ownership (TCO) over three years:

- **Off-the-shelf:** subscription × users × 36 months + widgets + integrator services.
- **Custom:** development + 36 months of servers + support (if needed).

For a small team with a standard sales process, off-the-shelf is usually cheaper. As users grow, processes get more complex and integrations appear, a custom CRM starts paying back.

## How to keep CRM costs down

1. **Start with the most painful process.** The first module — usually requests and the customer base — pays back fast.
2. **Roll out in stages.** Invest in the next module once the first has delivered.
3. **Drop what you do not need.** A clickable prototype shows what is really required before development starts.
4. **Reuse what you have.** Integrate with 1C and other systems instead of replacing them.

## Conclusion

The cost of a CRM is not the subscription or development price but the total cost over three years — and the value it brings. Off-the-shelf CRM is good for a quick start; custom CRM suits distinctive processes and growing teams.
